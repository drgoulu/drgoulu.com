After a decade-long hiatus on this blog, I wondered what modern channels are best suited to promote it.

I left Twitter when Musk bought it to turn it into a personal propaganda megaphone, and then opened an [account on Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/goulu.bsky.social). It looks like a promising network, but it doesn't drive much traffic for now...

And then, almost without thinking, I imported my ancient [OPML](w:OPML) file into the [Vivaldi feed reader](https://vivaldi.com/features/feed-reader/), and discovered to my amazement that the websites I used to follow are still actively publishing via [RSS](w:RSS).

Even [drgoulu.com's feed](https://drgoulu.com/index.xml#feed) was still alive, though frozen in 2015 due to a minor Hugo glitch. It's now fixed: you can once again subscribe to drgoulu.com in your favorite RSS reader (personally, I find the one built into Vivaldi quite satisfying).

Here is a curated selection of blogs and sites I am (re-)following this way:

- [David Madore's Weblog](http://www.madore.org/~david/weblog/): an eclectic mathematician writing in-depth, rigorously documented opinion pieces in a deliberately vintage aesthetic that forces you to focus on substance over style.
- [Greg Egan's website](https://www.gregegan.net): mathematician and author of (very) hard science-fiction. I tried reading [Diaspora](w:Diaspora_(novel)), but it proved a bit too hard-sci-fi for me (see [excerpt](https://www.gregegan.net/DIASPORA/01/Orphanogenesis.html)). Yet I thoroughly enjoy his website, teeming with mathematical oddities like [Constant Weight Rollercoasters](https://www.gregegan.net/SCIENCE/Rollercoasters/Rollercoasters.html), which explores how to design a rollercoaster where riders experience a constant apparent weight, complete with neat graphs:
[![](https://www.gregegan.net/SCIENCE/Rollercoasters/cyclotron.gif)](https://www.gregegan.net/SCIENCE/Rollercoasters/Rollercoasters.html)
- Another mathematician's blog: [John D. Cook](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/). He frequently covers number theory and cryptography. Among other gems, see [Manipulating a random number generator](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2017/08/16/manipulating-a-random-number-generator/), a mind-boggling post where he shows how to rig a pseudo-random number generator so it produces a predetermined sequence—specifically the ASCII codes of his own name—after a set number of iterations.
- [Zythom's blog](https://zythom.fr): (former) IT specialist and judicial expert. Always insightful, profoundly humane, and sensitive. I hope his latest post isn't a final farewell...
- [XKCD](https://xkcd.com/3308/): the inimitable scientific webcomic, cited across drgoulu [here](/2016/05/08/et-si-le-livre-de-xkcd/), [here](/2012/09/05/un-petit-pas-pour-lhomme/), and [here](/2019/07/31/est-ce-qu-un-ouvrier-est-deja-tombe-dans-une-piscine-a-refroidissement-d-une-centrale-nucleaire-et-en-est-sorti-vivant/).
- [Schneier on Security](https://www.schneier.com): the website of [Bruce Schneier](w:Bruce_Schneier), my go-to security guru (in IT and beyond) ever since I read [Liars and Outliers](/2013/08/25/liars-and-outliers/).
- [AFIS](https://www.afis.org): the French Association for Scientific Information, tirelessly combating pseudoscience, disinformation, and scientific illiteracy.
- [Freakonometrics](https://freakonometrics.hypotheses.org): Arthur Charpentier's blog on statistics, with whom I had several exchanges regarding the [Gini coefficient](/2009/03/21/combien-dinegalite/) and [electoral fraud detection](/2012/12/07/fraudez-benford/).
