The first "scientific" article of the new DrGoulu.com is dedicated to a thrilling documentary about the citizen group that painstakingly exposed the scientific fraud committed by [Didier Raoult](w:Didier_Raoult) and his team while promoting [hydroxychloroquine](w:Hydroxychloroquine) during the Covid-19 pandemic.

{{< youtube "i2jpMckCzZM" >}}

This four-part documentary series is currently streaming on HBO, but will undoubtedly air on public channels soon as it is produced by [Bonne Pioche](w:Bonne_Pioche). One thing is certain: if you are interested in how science actually works—for better or worse—do not miss it.

I'll "spoil" a little bit below, but more as a teaser; you decide whether to read on...

The documentary highlights two major real-world shortcomings of modern scientific research:

1. **The publication rat race**: As mentioned in the final episode, Raoult co-authored over 3,500 papers (!!!) throughout his career, or roughly 3 per week (!!!). Through sheer volume and bureaucratic funding formulas, this brought in 10 million euros in grants to his research unit without any real assessment of the quality of this deluge of papers.
2. **The papal infallibility of high-profile scientific luminaries** whose ego expands alongside their age. Much like Luc Montagnier with his "water memory" or, to some extent, J.P. Petit in cosmology, some scientists forget the humility and caution inherent to their craft. They succumb to the temptation of abusing their notoriety to push for a Nobel Prize or a permanent place in the history books.

It also shows that in such situations, peer reviewers and politicians are rarely the ones sounding the alarm. They are either too conflicted, or simply indifferent.

Raoult, his cohort tasked with harassing his critics on social media, the then-Health Minister, and others all made the same fundamental mistake: claiming (or believing) that only a world-renowned infectious disease specialist could spot that Raoult's papers were worthless.

In truth, all it takes is an understanding of basic scientific methodology (the kind acquired during a PhD), middle-school level math and statistics, and critical thinking skills to detect glaring anomalies in a scientific paper.

And with modern communication tools, a handful of people sharing the same concerns can easily form a "gang." In this case, they were:

- **Guillaume Limousin**: Mathematics teacher (and former researcher).
- **Alexander Samuel**: High-school teacher, PhD in biology, and heavy metal vocalist.
- **Fabrice Frank**: IT business owner and avid surfer.
- **Samuel**: Scientific researcher holding a PhD (his surname is not disclosed in the documentary).
- **Frédérique Delestaing**: Nurse practicing in Marseille.
- **Magali Carcopino-Tusoli**: Internal medicine physician in Marseille.

And this group of ordinary citizens turned scientific [Winkelrieds](w:Arnold_von_Winkelried) dubbed themselves "the cherry scooters" (*les trottinettes cerises*) in reference to:

1. The title of an early video where Raoult claimed: "Coronavirus: fewer deaths than from scooter accidents."
2. "[Cherry picking](w:Cherry_picking)": the unfortunately natural human tendency to select only data that supports a pet hypothesis while discarding data that contradicts it.

The documentary illustrates very well how the strength of this group derived from their complementary skills and unity against adversaries, if not outright enemies.

Because what stunned me the most was the sheer ferocity of the "pro-Raoult" mob, who hounded critics across social media to the point of driving people toward suicide, issued death threats, and even resorted to physical assaults.

It's a phenomenon previously seen with fans of [Nikola Tesla](/2012/08/19/nikola-tesla-genie-mais-connu/), [Montagnier](/2020/04/18/pensez-vous-que-le-professeur-montagner-prix-nobel-de-medecine-2008-est-devenu-complotiste-en-affirmant-que-le-covid-19-a-ete-fabrique-par-l-etre-humain-voir-la-video-youtube/), or JPP: hordes of people without any scientific background turn a scientist into an untouchable, quasi-religious guru.

In Raoult's case, the Covid-19 climate led many desperate individuals to see him as a providential savior, particularly since, riding his prior prestige, he promised everything would return to normal by summer 2020, with no second wave and no vaccines needed...

Contradicting Raoult meant denying the existence of a simple, cheap miracle cure, while supposedly supporting lockdowns and Big Pharma vaccines. This drove many uninformed people into becoming aggressive and cruel...

The best piece of news in the documentary is that one initially pro-Raoult follower was eventually convinced by the gang's mathematical arguments and joined their side. So changing minds is possible...

One minor criticism I'd offer of the documentary is that it glosses over the middle-school-level statistical blunders that were quickly spotted in the publications (beyond the tiny sample size of the first study). Given that court judges were able to comprehend them [1], audiences could have understood them too...

Another slight omission is failing to mention [Elisabeth Bik](w:Elisabeth_Bik), a researcher specialized in detecting scientific fraud, particularly through duplicated and manipulated images. She too uncovered anomalies, was sued by Raoult, and received such widespread community support that he eventually dropped the lawsuit.

Ultimately, Raoult's obstinacy expelled him from the pantheon of French science. Once hailed by President Emmanuel Macron as a "great scientist," he has landed straight in the trash bin of science and history.
