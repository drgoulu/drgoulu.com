*Réponse publiée [sur Quora](https://www.quora.com/Is-there-a-supermassive-black-hole-in-the-middle-of-the-milky-way/answer/Dr-Goulu)*

Yes. Look at this:

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k7xl_zjz0o8)

It’s the recorded positions of stars at the center of the Milky Way for 10 years. (more data are available now[[1]](#ckFtd) but the recent video is not as “raw” as this one)

You can see the stars, especially the [S2 star](w:en:S2_(star)) orbiting “something” invisible, and weighting about 4 million sun masses. S2 reaches 2.5% of the speed of light, faster than any man made object. And it’s a large star, weighting about 14 sun masses !

There’s nothing else than a supermassive black hole that can produce this gravitational pull.

Our central supermassive blackhole is called [Sagittarius A*](w:en:Sagittarius_A*), and the [Event Horizon Telescope](w:en:Event_Horizon_Telescope) will release images of its immediate surroundings at the end of this year. Researchers are quite impatient to see its [Accretion disk](w:en:Accretion_disk).
