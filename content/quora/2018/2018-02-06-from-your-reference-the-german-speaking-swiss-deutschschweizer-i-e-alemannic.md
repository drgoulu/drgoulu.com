*Réponse publiée [sur Quora](https://www.quora.com/How-can-you-be-German-and-Swiss-at-the-same-time-isnt-that-identity-crisis/answer/Dr-Goulu)*

From your reference “The **German-speaking Swiss**(*Deutschschweizer*), i.e. [Alemannic German](https://www.quora.com/wiki/Alemannic_German), historically amalgamated from the [Gallo-Roman](https://www.quora.com/wiki/Gallo-Roman) population and the [Alemanni](https://alemanni.quora.com/) and [Burgundii](https://www.quora.com/wiki/Burgundii), including subgroups such as the [Walser](https://www.quora.com/wiki/Walser)” (and “Alemannic German” is a pleonasm : in their own language its [Alemannisch](w:als:Alemannisch))

So you must go back about one millenia to find connections between Swiss German and modern Germany, and it's not related to emigration or colonisation
